## Technical issues

- Orbit: The statement that the spacecraft has a constant supply of sunlight is incorrect: sun-synchronous orbit fixes local solar time of node passage but does not eliminate orbital eclipses.
- Orbit: A sun-synchronous orbit does not pass over almost the entire Earth in the broad sense implied; its ground-track latitude coverage is limited by its near-polar inclination, and revisit of a specific reef is not established.
- Orbit: The listed RAAN of 27.33° is not determined by the launch facility's location. Launch latitude constrains achievable inclination; RAAN depends on launch timing and desired orbital-plane orientation.
- Orbit: The claim that the circular orbit has no perigee is imprecise: perigee is not uniquely defined for a circular orbit, as are argument of perigee and true anomaly; argument of latitude is a valid substitute when inclination is non-equatorial.
- Ground Control & Communications: The access report contains a duplicated access entry and apparent ordering/date inconsistencies, so its claimed access frequency and mean duration need validation. Contact is not guaranteed for the vast majority of passes merely because the orbit is near-polar.
- Mission Duration & EOL: A drag sail at 600 km may reduce orbital lifetime, but deorbit time and compliance with disposal requirements are not estimated. The report also says the satellite has no thrusters, so the claimed EOL manoeuvre should be described as passive decay rather than an orbital manoeuvre.
- Launch: Vega E was a future, unbuilt launcher in the report's stated timeframe; the proposed 2025 ride is speculative and does not establish a confirmed launch opportunity or compatibility with the required orbit.
- Introduction: The report's claim that ocean acidity has increased by 30 percent since the industrial revolution is not clearly defined or supported as written; ocean surface acidity change is commonly expressed as an increase in acidity rather than this unsupported percentage.
