## Technical issues

- Launch: Vega E is presented as a likely 2025 launch vehicle, but Vega E was not an operational launcher and did not make its first launch in 2024; this undermines the proposed launch plan. The stated future launch schedule and rideshare availability are not substantiated.
- Launch: The claim that two launch windows per day follow because the launch-site latitude is lower than the target inclination is oversimplified. Actual launch azimuth, range-safety constraints, target RAAN and rideshare orbit determine feasible windows.
- Orbit: At 600 km altitude, a near-circular orbit has a period of about 97 minutes, but the report does not give a period or demonstrate that the selected RAAN and launch date can deliver the stated elements. RAAN is not determined simply by the launch facility location.
- Orbit: The report says the orbit has a constant sun angle and provides uniform illumination, but does not specify local solar time of the ascending/descending node. SSO fixes local solar time at a given latitude; it does not guarantee constant illumination conditions for every imaging pass.
- Orbit: A single 600 km spacecraft cannot image the entire 250 km-wide reef at adequate resolution merely by selecting this altitude. Sensor swath, optical resolution, pointing, cloud cover and revisit/coverage requirements are not specified or analysed.
- Ground Control & Communications: Mount Pleasant Radio Observatory is identified as a radio astronomy facility, but the report does not establish that it has suitable spacecraft TT&C antennas, frequency authorizations, or operational control capability. Pass access alone does not demonstrate a usable communications link.
- Ground Control & Communications: The access table includes a duplicated access entry and a first listed interval with no access number; the claim of frequent contact is not reconciled with the reported gaps between passes or the time window represented.
- Mission Duration & EOL: The report first says an EOL orbital manoeuvre occurs, then states the spacecraft has no thrusters and relies on a passive drag sail. A deployable drag sail is a plausible concept but requires design, reliability and lifetime/deorbit analysis; none is provided.
- Orbit: The report describes a circular orbit as having undefined argument of perigee and true anomaly, but does not give a fully specified nonsingular alternative set including a reference epoch and phase; the stated argument of latitude alone is insufficient for reproducibility.
