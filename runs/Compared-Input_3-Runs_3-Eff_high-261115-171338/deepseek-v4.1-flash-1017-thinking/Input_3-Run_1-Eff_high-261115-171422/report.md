## Technical issues

- The Most Ethical Path: The NHS IT-failure case is called an 'almost identical malfunction scenario' without evidence that it is technically equivalent to the server defects.
- DICUSSION: To Ship or Not to Ship: No quantitative risk assessment of failure likelihood, severity or test coverage distinguishes possible physical harm from higher-probability data-loss failures.

- DICUSSION: To Ship or Not to Ship: Speculative claims that a competitor would ship defective products and that Rachel would lose her job are not supported by evidence.
- The Most Ethical Path: EACE clause 2.3b concerns individual competence, not company resource or time constraints, so its application to the six-month QA window is questionable.
- References: Reference list is incomplete and inconsistent: BBC item [3] lacks a URL, [1] URL is malformed, and [4] author formatting is unclear.
- DICUSSION: To Ship or Not to Ship: The required trade-off about large-scale testing informing future safety improvements is not discussed.

- DICUSSION: To Ship or Not to Ship: Grammar errors such as 'The economic benefits of could very well' and 'have her company might go under' reduce clarity.
- INTRODUCTION: The chip contract company is listed as a stakeholder but its interests and risks are not analysed.
