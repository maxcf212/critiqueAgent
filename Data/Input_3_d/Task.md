## Task
For this assessment, students will submit a report on an engineering ethics case inspired by real world
scenarios faced by engineering professionals.
Students will  identify  and discuss ethical  issues  associated  with  their scenario. They  should  explore
these  ethical  dilemmas  and  consider  how stakeholders might  make  decisions  regarding  them. When
doing  this,  students should  identify and  discuss issues and  considerations  both for  and  against  the
decisions, using  reputable  evidence/sources  and conclude the  report with  a  justified recommended
course of action. Only describing that a particular action should be taken just because a law or regulation
said so is insufficient. The conclusions drawn should be the culmination of thorough research into the
ethical considerations   for   all   involved   stakeholders,   and   a   holistic   discussion of   the   potential
consequences of their decisions. The report task is summarised as:

Discuss how stakeholders might make decisions about
ethical issues associated with your scenario.

## Format
The report must include an abstract of around 150 words. The report should also include an introduction,
body and conclusion. You may use headings to organise the body of the report. There are no restrictions
on  how  you  organise  the  body  of  your  report,  although  students  may  refer  to  the  marking  criteria  for
guidance. Figures  and  tables  may  be  used.  The  total  length  of  the  introduction,  body  and  conclusion
should  be  fewer  than 1,500 words. References  should  be  made  using  consistent and  established
referencing  styles,  such  as  Harvard  or  IEEE. References  and  figures  do  not  count  towards  your  word
count.

## Resources
You may refer to relevant course lectures and workshops. The Engineers Australia, Code of Ethics may
be worth referencing in your report.


## Topic 2: To Ship or Not to Ship
A quality assurance engineer must decide whether or not to ship products that might be defective.
Rachel works as a Quality Assurance Engineer at a large electronics company. She is responsible for the
final testing of her company’s servers and is part of a team which decides when new products will be
shipped to distributors for sale.
Rachel’s company has a contract with another company which makes the chips which are incorporated
into the servers Rachel’s company makes. The business model for this product is to release a new
generation server approximately every six months, meaning Rachel has a limited time frame to conduct
her Quality Control tests.
Because there is such a short amount of time between the release of each next new product, the Quality
and Assurance department cannot perform every possible test on the servers to ensure they are defect
free. Rachel will not ship a product if there is any possibility that the server could malfunction and cause
physical harm to the customer. However, she will ship a product that has a higher likelihood of failure
resulting in data loss for the customer, because she knows that if she doesn't, her company's competitor
will.
Reference: Clare Bartlett was a 2014-2015 Hackworth Fellow in Engineering Ethics at the Markkula Center
for  Applied  Ethics  at  Santa  Clara  University.  August  26, 2015. https://www.scu.edu/ethics/focus-
areas/more-focus-areas/engineering-ethics/engineering-ethics-cases/to-ship-or-not-to-ship/
Rachel learns that a large contract of servers is being procured by a government health department –
meaning failure of these devices can lead directly to improper health administration for hospitals and
emergency  services,  and  catastrophic  consequences  for  the  business.  However,  Rachel  knows  that
fulfilling this contract will provide significant revenue and growth for the business which can positively
impact her employment. Furthermore, distributing and testing these servers at such scale will allow the
product engineers to make safety design improvements for even larger international contracts planned
in a few years' time.
Ensure your response addresses the following:
- Is this an ethical way to conduct business?
- How should she determine when to ship a product with known defects? What are the trade-offs
in decision making?
- What should Rachel do?
Note: you must reference external case studies to support all your arguments.
