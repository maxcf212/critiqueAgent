AERO9500 Final Report (Part 2)

T3 2020 Assignment

In this assignment you will design a space mission and identify the main elements of its

space segment. The intent is to demonstrate your understanding of the course material

by applying what you have learned about the space segment systems (launch, orbits

etc.) by developing a preliminary plan for a new mission.

The mission should benefit Australia in some way. Try to keep your mission concept

simple. Some ideas include:

- 1. A bushfire tracking and alert system

- 2. Weather satellite

- 3. Internet / communication

- 4. Earth observation for city planning / real estate

You will use the software of your choice to complete the design, although you are

encouraged to supplement the design with hand calculations as needed. Your software

options include:

- 1. STK – this is industry-standard mission design software. There are self-guided tutorials available for you to get started learning how to use it. STK is available in the MECH computer lab 204. You can access it in person (keep in mind there is a limit to the number of students allowed in the lab), or via remote access. You can also download a demo version.

- 2. PIGI – this is software developed by Saber Astronautics, an Australian company. If you are using PIGI, you can enter your design in their competition. In any case, you will need to register for the competition in order to get a license for the software.

- 3. Other software, e.g. flightclub - please contact Naomi to discuss before you start working with other software.

There are two parts to this assignment. Part 1 is a preliminary report (which you have already done) designed to help


guide you in your completion of the main assignment in Part 2.

Part 2

In this final assignment report you will present your mission design and all related work.

You should include at least the following information:

- 1. What is the spacecraft’s primary mission?

- 2. Will you use one or multiple spacecraft?

- 3. Who are the users (operator and customers)?

- 4. Where will it launch from, and using which launch vehicle?

- 5. What type of orbit will you use? What are its characteristics and orbital elements?

- 6. Will you need to perform any orbital maneuvers?

- 7. Which ground control facilities will you use? How often will your spacecraft be able to communicate with ground control?

- 8. How long will the mission last, and what are your plans for EOL?

Your assignment is to be submitted as a technical report (single pdf document) via

Turnitin on Moodle. The writing style should be clear, effective and conform to that in

general use in engineering reports. Correct grammar and spelling should be used

throughout.

The overall structure of the report should be clear and logical. Your report must be

concise, with a page limit of 20 pages for the core part (i.e. excluding title page, table of

contents, references, etc).

Include with your report (or in the appendix) relevant screenshots, and all relevant STK

or PIGI files.

The report should have the following sections:

- Executive summary

- Introduction - mission overview and rationale

- Main body [All sections describing the mission's space segment, e.g. launch, orbit, ground stations, coverage, mission operation and end-of-life]


• Conclusion

• Bibliography

Figures and tables should be fully and correctly labelled, e.g., Figure number, title and

(if necessary) a reference.

All text, diagrams, ideas or other information which did not originate with you should be

clearly identified and the sources properly cited and referenced. Note that Wikipedia is

not considered a proper source (although it can be useful as a starting point). The report

will be checked for plagiarism.

Refer to the course outline and marking rubric (which will be made available on Moodle)

for more information.

The final report is due on Moodle by Friday of Week 10, 20 November at 17:00 and is

worth 25% of your course grade.
